## Implementation and evidence

`src/openai_ns_reconstruction/original_q4_integral_coverage_guard_v1.py` wraps an injected integrator. It accepts exact integer/Fraction endpoints, singleton increasing bounds and a bounded positive partition count. It records each full cell passed to the callback and its returned interval, checks the callback and declared cells agree in order, checks their exact contiguous coverage, and separately recomputes the lower and upper rational sums of cell width times callback enclosure. The result must contain that entire recomputed interval; a wider result is kept unchanged. Raw scalars and selected points are rejected. Cell snapshots are checked again to detect changes during the call.

The result is `conditional_enclosure`, with `actual_value_selected=false` and `provider_certified=false`. Structural coverage and arithmetic checks do not prove that a callback encloses its function on a whole cell, that its source is the original provider, or that directed endpoints are certified. These claims assume the injected interval objects and providers honor their interface; the guard is not a security boundary against arbitrary mutable or malicious Python objects. The previous Q4 runtime retains its conservative `coverage_verified=false`; the new guard has not yet been wired to a live original-source runtime. Its integral signature is compatible, but an appropriate backend facade is needed because the runtime looks for a backend integrator first. The legacy `RangeIntegrator.integrate` returns a bare interval and therefore needs a separate full-enclosure adapter; it cannot be passed directly.

The single frozen entry used a fresh synthetic `g(x)=2x+3` on `[-2,1]`, six cells, and no ancestor calculation. All 18 fixture checks passed. The exact synthetic integral is 6, enclosed by `[9/2,15/2]`; a deliberately wider `[3/2,21/2]` was retained. Checks rejected missing or extra callbacks, incorrect declared partitions including a different valid partition, gaps, overlaps, reverse order, too-narrow sums, raw point inputs, and invalid bounds or partition counts. These are synthetic implementation results, not values or certified error bounds for m15.
