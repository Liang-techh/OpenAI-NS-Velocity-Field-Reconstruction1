# Anisotropic scale and local stress-cone route

The [OpenAI Navier–Stokes paper](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/32d9f210-8b73-45e0-91bc-82a30aef8a9a/navier-stokes.pdf) uses radial length `ell_r ~ tau^(1/2)` and axial length `ell_z ~ tau^(1/2-h)`, so `ell_z/ell_r ~ tau^(-h)`. Its chosen regime has `0<h<1/100`; the ST073 coordinate evaluator fixes `h=0.005` and `D=0.495`. Therefore the aspect ratio grows only by a factor `2^0.005` per halving of backward time. Doubling it requires about 200 halvings in this fixed-exponent model. The registered window `tau=.128` to `.0084` spans fewer than four halvings and predicts only about 1.4% relative growth, consistent with the measured support-to-peak-swirl-radius ratio `0.7281 → 0.7392`. The asymptotic paper statement does not establish that our finite-slab candidate reaches those later scales.

For the nonaxisymmetric pulse route, a positive stress covariance must lie in an admissible local cone before a wave can realize it. `cone_axial_shape_search.py` adds six exact-solenoidal radial/axial collar streamfunctions to the older fixed-radius physical background and searches their coefficients at five heights. The initial background passes the physical analog cone at one of five heights. The bounded six-mode search observed a best candidate passing three of five, but **none passing all five**; the soft-objective optimizer's final candidate passes none. Several lower heights retain `target·N>0` in that final candidate. This is a limited numerical search, not a mathematical impossibility result or the paper's normalized cone.

The important design consequence is that changing a few localized shapes on the present compact background has not repaired the stress orientation, just as similar fits failed whole-volume momentum holdouts. The next construction should supply a mean background with the paper's radial moment identities and heat exterior, compute its leading normalized tangential stress, and only then solve supported phase/amplitude equations for a nonaxisymmetric correction. The heat evaluator and kinematic bridges already in the repository can provide boundary traces, but their stress and transport mismatches remain unresolved. Merely pushing the current finite-slab fit to smaller `tau` or interpreting a positive local cone at one height as a wave solution would not satisfy the objective.

Reproduce the fixed-radius screen with `python experiments/root_st073/cone_axial_shape_search.py`; its output is `experiments/root_st073/compact_potential/cone_axial_shape_search.json` and retains `accepted: false`. The direct heat join is in `heat_join/screen.json`; radial moment diagnostics are in `compact_potential/moment_audit.json`.
