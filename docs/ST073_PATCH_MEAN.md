# ST073 compact mean correction and time-persistence screen

Run `python experiments/root_st073/curl_wave_patch_mean.py` from the repository root. The full numeric report is `experiments/root_st073/compact_potential/curl_wave_patch_mean.json`.

This extends the previous two-harmonic patch fit with a compact axisymmetric vector potential and pressure. At the registered endpoint, each potential is multiplied by `(tau-tau0)`; its curl is exactly divergence-free. The mean residual is fitted on the same 5 x 5 patch, with 16 interlaced held-out spatial nodes. The pressure and velocity coefficients are real, and the velocity has no value change at the endpoint, only a time derivative.

At wave amplitude `0.1`, the held-out sampled full-momentum maximum falls from `3,226,316` after harmonic correction to `2,801,804` after the mean correction. The original field's maximum on these nodes is `728,781`, so this candidate still worsens the field. A small-amplitude scan found that amplitude `0.005` plus mean correction has held-out maximum `345,332`; the axisymmetric correction alone has `301,213`. Both are improvements over the uncorrected field on these nodes, but the tiny nonaxisymmetric amplitude carries only `0.005^2 = 2.5e-5` of the target wave covariance. No such result is a whole-support bound.

The decisive failure is temporal. At one held-out point with amplitude `0.005`, the corrected residual norm increases from `172,580` at the endpoint to `8,804,074` halfway through the `1e-4`-long cutoff interval, while the uncorrected baseline there is `63,262`. The forced return of the potential to zero creates a large time-derivative residual. This rules out accepting the short Taylor taper even where an endpoint patch fit looks favorable. Direct finite-difference evaluation of the callable field at the registered endpoint agreed with the algebraic predictor to `0.0021` absolute for the amplitude `0.1` diagnostic.

The next construction must evolve phase, amplitudes, mean flow, and pressure through an actual pulse, then put cutoffs where the amplitudes are already small. The [OpenAI Navier--Stokes paper](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/32d9f210-8b73-45e0-91bc-82a30aef8a9a/navier-stokes.pdf) uses its amplitude-pressure system (7.5), exact-curl representation (7.38), and controls the cutoff tails in Section 9 by exponential smallness near pulse endpoints. This prototype has none of that endpoint smallness. It is rejected as a Navier--Stokes candidate and remains a diagnostic for designing the coupled pulse solve.
